# Rewrite Gates

Use these gates before returning any scientific paper rewrite.

## No-Waste Sentence Gate

Every final sentence must add at least one of the following:

- technical claim
- result
- assumption
- definition
- comparison
- mechanism
- interpretation
- limitation

Delete or rewrite sentences that:

- recap what the reader just read
- announce the next step without technical content
- repeat a prior sentence
- use first-person paper prose unless requested
- exist only to make the paragraph sound smooth

## Technical Tightness Gate

Every technical sentence must satisfy these checks:

- Comparisons name the baseline.
- Qualitative terms such as "small", "large", "strong", "weak", "similar", and "substantial" are tied to a quantity, scale, mechanism, or explicit comparator.
- Pronouns have clear referents; replace a pronoun with the noun when ambiguity is possible.
- Mechanistic statements name the mechanism.
- Model-specific claims are not written as universal claims.
- Novelty claims are softened unless the evidence is explicit.

## Professor-Voice Gate

Reject prose that sounds like a generic writing assistant. Avoid:

- inflated transitions
- promotional claims
- stock AI phrases
- decorative adjectives
- over-balanced sentence cadence
- broad claims unsupported by the paragraph

Prefer:

- concrete nouns
- direct technical claims
- natural variation in sentence length
- mechanism before flourish
- quantitative interpretation where possible

## Gomez Lab Rules

- Every paper should have one central sentence.
- Short papers should make one main point.
- Organize the paper to convince the reader, not to report chronology.
- Make one point per sentence.
- Remove junk words and junk constructs.
- Avoid ambiguity and redundancy.
- Complete comparisons.
- Use active voice when possible.
- Avoid "as described below", "as mentioned before", "on the other hand", "it is obvious", and "etc".

## Paper 2 Defaults

Use only when the active context is Paper 2:

- Main point: tissue compliance affects intrathecal drug transport, and the effects vary spatially along the spinal canal.
- Target journal: Fluids and Barriers of the CNS.
- Use "rigid-wall model" or "non-compliant model".
- Do not make "open-topology model" a main label.
- Frame in vivo comparisons as consistency checks, not validation.
- Do not overemphasize the effective tissue layer unless needed.
